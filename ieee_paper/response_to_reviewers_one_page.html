Response to Reviewers

Paper ID 93: Bellman-Guided Warm-Start Search for LSTM Configuration in ECG Forecasting

We thank the reviewers for identifying weaknesses in causal attribution, validation design, uncertainty reporting, scalability, data provenance, and related-work coverage. We revised the manuscript and reran a controlled five-seed experiment. Complete development traces are split before scaling/windowing; the official ECG5000 test partition remains untouched until final evaluation. Each search-selected architecture is reinitialized and trained under the same final Adam/early-stopping protocol, thereby removing inherited training age from the reported test comparison.

Reviewer 4

1. Negative transfer and local trapping. We now state explicitly that tensor-shape compatibility is only a loading-safety condition; it provides no theoretical guarantee of positive transfer, global optimality, or finite-horizon Q-learning convergence. A validation gate compares inherited and freshly initialized compatible candidates under identical three-epoch budgets and propagates only the lower-validation-MSE branch. We present this as an empirical rollback safeguard, not a theorem.

2. Decoupling controller, optimizer, reuse, and training age. We added five matched arms: Q+Adam+gated reuse, Random+Adam+gated reuse, Q+SGD+gated reuse, Q+Adam+cold start, and Q+Adam+direct reuse. Data partitions, seeds, 12-episode horizon, candidate budget, and reward are fixed. Common from-scratch final retraining isolates architecture selection from optimizer-state inheritance and effective training age.

3. Scalability and overhead. The present table has 18 states and 7 actions (126 entries). For window length w, input dimension d, width H, and depth L, recurrent computation is approximately O(wL(H2+dH)); multi-lead inputs increase the first-layer term linearly, while an additional discrete hyperparameter with m levels multiplies the tabular state count by m. Mean updates and wall times are now reported; gated Q uses 4,128 mean updates versus 3,320 for cold-start Q.

4. ECG5000 source. We added the UCR/UEA archive citation, the ECG5000 Zenodo record (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.11186692), and PhysioNet provenance. The manuscript identifies BIDMC CHF record chf07 and limits interpretation because beats from one source record are not independent patients.

5. Recent methods. Related work now discusses representative 2020--2026 forecasting architectures, including Informer, DLinear, PatchTST, TimesNet, and ModernTCN. We clarify that these methods are contextual comparators rather than claimed endpoint-equivalent ECG baselines unless rerun under the same split and objective.

Reviewer 8

Controlled experiments and seeds. All five ablations were repeated for seeds {11, 22, 33, 44, 55}. We report mean results with 95% Student-t confidence intervals and retain the official 4,500-beat test partition for one post-selection evaluation per arm. The experiment separates Q versus random selection, Adam versus SGD, and gated/direct/cold-start reuse.

Key corrected result. Q+Adam+gated reuse obtained test MSE 0.02355 ± 0.00162, MAE 0.08920 ± 0.00332, and R2 0.96161 ± 0.00265 (95% CI half-widths). Cold-start Q was numerically best (MSE 0.02288 ± 0.00092), but intervals overlap. We therefore removed the earlier causal/superiority claim: within the tested budget, the evidence does not establish a statistically resolved advantage for Q-learning or checkpoint inheritance.

Editorial and reproducibility corrections. The abstract, contributions, method, algorithm, experimental protocol, results, limitations, and conclusion were aligned with the controlled experiment. Seed lists, partition policy, stopping criteria, candidate/final budgets, compute counts, provenance, and uncertainty are now explicit. Claims are restricted to forecasting methodology rather than clinical validity.

All changes are incorporated in the revised manuscript; this response intentionally reports both positive and null findings.
