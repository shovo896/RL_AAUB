\title{Bellman-Guided Warm-Start Search for LSTM Configuration in ECG Forecasting}

\author{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Sequential hyperparameter searches often discard trained parameters between evaluations, even when consecutive candidates differ only locally. We investigate whether conservative checkpoint inheritance improves a small Q-learning search for LSTM-based electrocardiogram (ECG) forecasting. Hidden width, recurrent depth, and dropout define an 18-state Markov decision process with seven coordinate-wise actions. To separate controller, optimizer, and inheritance effects, we conduct five controlled search variants over five declared random seeds. Candidate evaluations receive three epochs, whereas each validation-selected architecture is reinitialized and trained under a common Adam protocol for at most 50 epochs with early stopping. The official ECG5000 test partition remains untouched until final evaluation. Q-learning with validation-gated reuse obtains test MSE $0.02355\pm0.00162$ and $R^2=0.9616\pm0.0026$ (mean and 95\% Student-$t$ interval half-width). A Q-learning cold-start search is numerically better at MSE $0.02288\pm0.00092$, while the intervals overlap. Random control, SGD-search, and direct-reuse variants also fall within a narrow performance band. Thus, the controlled evidence does not establish a statistically resolved advantage for Q-learning or checkpoint inheritance; instead, it shows that the earlier large gain was driven by jointly changing optimizer and effective training age. The principal contribution is a leakage-controlled, reproducible audit of Bellman-guided warm-start search and its computational trade-offs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Electrocardiograms (ECGs) couple abrupt local morphology with longer temporal dependencies, making them a demanding setting for sequential prediction. Open resources such as PhysioNet \cite{goldberger2000physiobank} and the UCR Time Series Archive \cite{dau2019ucr} support reproducible study of these signals. Although ECG5000 is typically used for classification, its traces also admit a self-supervised forecasting task: infer the next standardized amplitude from a fixed history. This task probes short-horizon temporal representation without treating the diagnostic class label as a target.

LSTM performance in this regime is governed not only by learned weights but also by architectural and optimization choices. Width controls state capacity, depth determines the recurrent hierarchy, and dropout regularizes inter-layer communication; optimizer and training duration alter the solution reached within any fixed architecture. Empirical comparisons of LSTM variants show that these apparently routine choices can dominate marginal changes to the cell design \cite{hochreiter1997lstm,greff2017search}. A search procedure that retrains every nearby configuration from scratch may therefore spend most of its budget relearning transferable structure.

Existing HPO strategies address evaluation cost in different ways. Random search is a strong baseline \cite{bergstra2012random}; Bayesian optimization uses a surrogate to prioritize informative evaluations \cite{snoek2012practical}; successive halving allocates progressively larger budgets to promising candidates \cite{jamieson2016nonstochastic,li2018hyperband}; and BOHB combines model-based proposal with bandit allocation \cite{falkner2018bohb}. RL-based NAS instead represents architecture design as a sequence of rewarded decisions \cite{zoph2017nas}. These approaches motivate a narrower question: when candidate LSTMs differ by a single local edit, can a transparent controller exploit compatible weights without obscuring what is being compared?

We study this question in the finite design space encoded by the supplied notebook. A tabular Q-learning agent modifies one hyperparameter at a time, and a strict shape test determines whether the next candidate inherits the preceding checkpoint. The resulting method is deliberately modest in scale, but fully inspectable. Its contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a finite-state formulation of LSTM width, depth, and dropout that exposes every controller transition;
    \item a validation-gated inheritance rule that compares compatible warm and cold candidates under equal per-episode budgets;
    \item a five-seed, one-factor-at-a-time ablation of controller, optimizer, and reuse, followed by common-protocol retraining; and
    \item a trace-disjoint development split and untouched official test evaluation with uncertainty and compute reporting.
\end{itemize}

The experiment is an algorithmic case study rather than a clinical evaluation. Its central result is deliberately conservative: after confounds are controlled, the tested search variants are not statistically separated by their 95\% intervals.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Deep Models for Time Series}
LSTM introduced gated memory as a mechanism for preserving gradients over long sequences \cite{hochreiter1997lstm}. Later empirical work showed that robust baselines and tuned components often matter more than increasingly elaborate gate variants \cite{greff2017search}. Hybrid architectures such as LSTM--FCN combine recurrent and convolutional pathways \cite{karim2018lstmfcn}, while large-scale evaluations across UCR datasets document substantial sensitivity to architecture and preprocessing \cite{fawaz2019review}. Forecasting surveys likewise conclude that recurrent models remain competitive when validation and model selection are handled carefully \cite{hewamalage2021rnn,lim2021survey}.

Recent forecasting research has broadened the architectural landscape. Informer targets efficient long-horizon attention \cite{zhou2021informer}; PatchTST tokenizes local temporal patches \cite{nie2023patchtst}; TimesNet represents temporal variation in two dimensions \cite{wu2023timesnet}; DLinear demonstrates that carefully designed linear baselines can rival substantially more complex models \cite{zeng2023dlinear}; and ModernTCN revisits convolution with enlarged effective receptive fields \cite{luo2024moderntcn}. These methods were evaluated primarily on long-horizon benchmarks, whereas our endpoint is one-step prediction from a length-20 ECG window. Their published numbers are therefore not directly comparable to ours. We use them to position the forecaster class, while the controlled empirical comparison addresses the paper's actual variable of interest---the search policy and reuse mechanism---under an identical LSTM family, split, and compute accounting.

Our forecaster is intentionally minimal: an LSTM maps a univariate window to its final hidden state, and a linear head predicts the subsequent sample. Dropout is searchable because it can limit co-adaptation \cite{srivastava2014dropout}; under the recurrent implementation used here, however, it acts only between stacked layers and is inactive for $L=1$.

\subsection{Sequential Configuration Search}
Bellman's dynamic-programming view of sequential decisions \cite{bellman1957markovian} underlies Q-learning, which estimates action values without an explicit transition model \cite{watkins1992q}. RL-based NAS applies this principle to architecture generation \cite{zoph2017nas}. ENAS reduces its cost through shared parameters \cite{pham2018enas}, while network-transformation methods preserve compatible weights as a graph evolves \cite{cai2018efficient}. Such reuse is computationally attractive, but it couples a candidate's measured quality to its optimization history.

The present search occupies a deliberately restricted point between black-box HPO and full NAS. Each action edits at most one coordinate of a fixed LSTM family, so the transition graph is explicit and the value function remains tabular. Checkpoint reuse is sequential rather than supernet-based and is permitted only when all parameter shapes agree; Adam \cite{kingma2015adam} then performs the short local update. This restriction sacrifices architectural breadth, but makes the interaction between controller state, model compatibility, and accumulated training directly auditable.

Unlike claims based on an unmatched end-to-end baseline, our confirmatory experiment compares Q-learning with a matched random controller and independently changes the search optimizer and reuse policy. All selected architectures are subsequently reinitialized and trained by the same final optimizer. This two-level design distinguishes search-path quality from the advantage of carrying older parameters into the reported predictor.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Problem Statement}
Let $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ and $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{val}}$ denote the training and validation sets of input windows and next-sample targets. For an LSTM configuration $\lambda=(H,L,D)$, training procedure $\mathcal{A}$, and parameters $\theta$, the validation objective is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(\lambda,\theta)
  =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{val}}|}
  \sum_{(\mathbf{X},y)\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{val}}}
  \left(y-f_{\lambda,\theta}(\mathbf{X})\right)^2.
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
Conventional HPO seeks $\lambda^*=\arg\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(\lambda,\mathcal{A}(\lambda))$ under an implicit independence assumption: each $\lambda$ is trained without reference to previous candidates. Checkpoint inheritance violates this assumption. The observed loss becomes path-dependent because it reflects both the current architecture and the optimization history carried into it. We therefore represent search as a finite-horizon decision process with return
\begin{equation}
   G_k=\sum_{j=0}^{K-k-1}\gamma^j r_{k+j+1},
   \qquad r_{k+1}=-\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val},k+1},
   \label{eq:return}
\end{equation}
over $K=12$ episodes. Search selects the architecture associated with minimum validation MSE rather than the terminal exploratory state. The selected architecture is then reinitialized and trained by the common final protocol, so inherited parameters and optimizer age cannot enter the reported test predictor.

\subsection{Forecasting Task and LSTM}
Let $\mathbf{x}=(x_1,\ldots,x_{140})$ be a standardized ECG trace. With window length $w=20$, each valid position yields the supervised pair
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{X}_{t}=[x_{t-w},\ldots,x_{t-1}],\qquad y_t=x_t.
    \label{eq:window}
\end{equation}
The LSTM consumes $\mathbf{X}_{t}$ sequentially. Its gated update can be written compactly as
\begin{align}
 \mathbf{i}_t &= \sigma(W_i x_t+U_i\mathbf{h}_{t-1}+\mathbf{b}_i), \nonumber\\
 \mathbf{f}_t &= \sigma(W_f x_t+U_f\mathbf{h}_{t-1}+\mathbf{b}_f), \nonumber\\
 \mathbf{o}_t &= \sigma(W_o x_t+U_o\mathbf{h}_{t-1}+\mathbf{b}_o), \nonumber\\
 \tilde{\mathbf{c}}_t &= \tanh(W_c x_t+U_c\mathbf{h}_{t-1}+\mathbf{b}_c), \nonumber\\
 \mathbf{c}_t &= \mathbf{f}_t\odot\mathbf{c}_{t-1}+\mathbf{i}_t\odot\tilde{\mathbf{c}}_t, \quad
 \mathbf{h}_t=\mathbf{o}_t\odot\tanh(\mathbf{c}_t),
 \label{eq:lstm}
\end{align}
The final hidden state is mapped to $\hat y_t=\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{h}_t+b$ by a linear regression head. Candidate quality is measured by
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{MSE}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(y_n-\hat y_n)^2,
 \quad
 \operatorname{MAE}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|y_n-\hat y_n|.
 \label{eq:metrics}
\end{equation}

\subsection{LSTM Configuration as an MDP}
The controller state $s=(H,L,D)$ records the hidden width, number of recurrent layers, and dropout rate:
\begin{equation}
H\in\{32,64,128\},\quad L\in\{1,2\},\quad D\in\{0,0.2,0.4\}.
\end{equation}
The Cartesian product contains 18 states. Seven actions increase or decrease a single coordinate, or preserve the current configuration. Boundary clipping maps infeasible outward moves to self-transitions. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} summarizes the resulting closed loop: the deterministic transition $T(s,a)$ configures the forecaster, whose validation loss supplies the controller reward.

After the LSTM specified by $s'=T(s,a)$ is trained, the environment returns $r=-\operatorname{MSE}_{\mathrm{val}}$. The agent then applies the one-step Q-learning update
\begin{equation}
 Q(s,a)\leftarrow Q(s,a)+\alpha\left[r+\gamma\max_{a'}Q(s',a')-Q(s,a)\right],
 \label{eq:qlearning}
\end{equation}
with learning rate $\alpha=0.4$ and discount factor $\gamma=0.8$. An $\epsilon$-greedy policy ($\epsilon=0.5$) alternates uniformly random actions with greedy selection. All action values are initialized to zero; the trajectory begins at $(64,1,0)$ and terminates after 12 episodes.

\subsection{Compatible-Weight Reuse}
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} details a search episode. After constructing the candidate specified by $s'$, the procedure compares every state-dictionary tensor with the preceding checkpoint. Shape agreement is only a memory-safety condition: it ensures that a checkpoint can be loaded, but it cannot guarantee positive transfer, global optimality, or Q-learning convergence in a 12-step horizon. We therefore introduce a validation gate. Whenever shapes match, an inherited branch and a freshly initialized branch receive the same three-epoch budget; the lower-validation-MSE branch becomes the next checkpoint. Any mismatch triggers a cold start directly. This empirical rollback rule prevents an observed harmful transfer from propagating, but it is not a theoretical proof that negative transfer is impossible. In contrast to ENAS-style supernet sharing \cite{pham2018enas}, reuse remains local in time and no parameters are shared concurrently across candidates.

The reported predictor is additionally decoupled from search age. After 12 episodes, the architecture with minimum validation MSE is selected, all inherited parameters and optimizer state are discarded, and the architecture is trained from scratch with Adam for at most 50 epochs. Early stopping with patience five restores the best validation checkpoint. Consequently, test performance reflects a common final-training protocol rather than unequal checkpoint age accumulated during search.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Warm-Start Q-Learning for LSTM Configuration}
\label{alg:warmstart}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$, $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{val}}$, state set $\mathcal{S}$, actions $\mathcal{U}$
\Ensure Best checkpoint $b^*$ and episode log $\mathcal{B}$
\State Set $s_0=(64,1,0)$, $Q(s,a)=0$, $\theta^-\gets\varnothing$, $\mathcal{B}\gets\varnothing$
\For{$k=1,\ldots,12$}
    \State Select $a_k$ by an $\epsilon$-greedy policy, $\epsilon=0.5$
    \State Set $s_{k+1}\gets T(s_k,a_k)$ and construct its LSTM
    \State If shapes match, train inherited and fresh branches for three epochs; otherwise train the fresh branch
    \State Retain the branch with lower validation MSE
    \State Evaluate RMSE, MAE, and $R^2$ on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{val}}$
    \State Set $r_k\gets-\mathrm{MSE}_k$ and update $Q$ using \eqref{eq:qlearning}
    \State Save the checkpoint; append $(s_{k+1},a_k,r_k)$ and metrics to $\mathcal{B}$
\EndFor
\State Select $\lambda^*\gets\arg\min_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{val}}(b)$
\State Reinitialize $f_{\lambda^*}$; train with common Adam/early stopping
\State Evaluate the restored checkpoint once on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{test}}$
\State \Return $f_{\lambda^*}$ and search log $\mathcal{B}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Protocol}
ECG5000 is distributed through the UCR/UEA Time Series Classification Archive \cite{dau2019ucr,ecg5000zenodo2024}. The archive documents that the 5,000 beats originate from the PhysioNet BIDMC Congestive Heart Failure record \texttt{chf07} \cite{goldberger2000physiobank}; individual beats were extracted, length-normalized to 140 samples, and automatically annotated. We retain the archive's official 500/4,500 train/test partition. For each seed, the 500 development traces are divided 80/20 into trace-disjoint training and validation sets, stratified by the supplied class label when feasible. Labels are used only for stratification, never as forecasting targets. A scalar standardizer is fitted exclusively to training-trace samples and then applied to validation and test traces. Windows are generated only after partitioning, preventing overlapping windows from a trace from crossing development subsets. The official test split is not accessed by the controller, early stopping, or architecture selection.

Five arms share the state space, action set, reward, per-candidate epoch budget, data partitions, and seeds: Q + Adam + gated reuse (primary), random + Adam + gated reuse (controller ablation), Q + SGD + gated reuse (search-optimizer ablation), Q + Adam + cold start (reuse ablation), and Q + Adam + direct reuse (gate ablation). After search, every selected architecture is reinitialized with the same seed offset and optimized by the common final protocol. We report means and two-sided 95\% Student-$t$ interval half-widths over five seeds.

In addition to \eqref{eq:metrics}, the recorded metrics are
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{RMSE}=\sqrt{\operatorname{MSE}},\qquad
 R^2=1-\frac{\sum_n(y_n-\hat y_n)^2}
 {\sum_n(y_n-\bar y)^2}.
 \label{eq:rmser2}
\end{equation}
MSE serves as both the training-aligned selection objective and the controller reward. MAE provides a complementary linear-error measure, while RMSE restores the scale of the standardized target. Test MSE, RMSE, MAE, and $R^2$ are aggregated independently across seeds; intervals are computed from the seed-level measurements rather than treating correlated windows as independent replicates.

\subsection{Model Size and Evaluation Budget}
For scalar input and output, a one-layer LSTM contains
$4H^2+13H+1$ trainable parameters. A two-layer model has
$12H^2+21H+1$, including both recurrent layers and the linear head. As Table~\ref{tab:params} shows, the design space ranges from 4,513 to 199,297 parameters. Width and depth therefore change the memory and computational requirements substantially; dropout changes neither the parameter count nor the tensor shapes.

With 20,000 training windows and a batch size of 512, each epoch contains 40 mini-batches, including one partial batch, so an ungated episode performs 120 updates. A gated compatible transition evaluates two branches and therefore performs 240. Across seeds, the observed total---including common final retraining---ranges from 3,320 mean updates for cold start to 4,224 for random gated reuse; the primary gated-Q arm averages 4,128 updates. Corresponding CPU wall times are reported with the results. The gate is therefore a safety mechanism with measurable overhead rather than free protection.

For input dimension $d$, recurrent width $H$, $L$ layers, and window length $w$, a forward/backward pass scales approximately as $O(wL(H^2+dH))$. Moving from one ECG lead to $d$ leads increases the first-layer input term linearly, while recurrent $H^2$ terms dominate at larger widths. The present Q table has only $18\times7=126$ entries and is negligible in memory; however, adding $m$ values for a new discrete hyperparameter multiplies the state count by $m$. Thus, tabular control scales poorly with an expanded architectural action space even though the current 18-state controller is inexpensive. Larger multi-lead studies should replace the table or factor the action representation, while reporting device memory, latency, and energy alongside error.

\section{Results and Discussion}
\subsection{Controlled Ablation on the Untouched Test Split}
Table~\ref{tab:controlled} reports the confirmatory results. The primary Q + Adam + gated-reuse arm reaches MSE $0.02355\pm0.00162$, MAE $0.08920\pm0.00332$, and $R^2=0.9616\pm0.0026$. Replacing Q-learning with a matched random controller changes mean MSE by only $8.8\times10^{-4}$. Replacing Adam by SGD during search changes it by $1.42\times10^{-3}$. Neither contrast is resolved by the corresponding 95\% intervals.

Most importantly, removing reuse does not degrade the final predictor. Q + Adam + cold start obtains the lowest mean MSE, $0.02288\pm0.00092$, and highest mean $R^2$, $0.9627\pm0.0015$. Direct reuse and validation-gated reuse are likewise close. Because every selected architecture was retrained from scratch with the same final optimizer, these results compare the configurations discovered by each search rather than checkpoints of unequal age. The earlier tenfold contrast between manual SGD/cold start and Adam/warm start is therefore not retained as evidence for RL or reuse.

\subsection{Negative Transfer, Search Stability, and Cost}
The gate never supplies a theorem against negative transfer; it supplies an observable counterfactual whenever inheritance is shape-compatible. Its practical cost is clear: the primary gated arm averages 808 more gradient updates than cold start. Mean wall times are $175.2$~s for gated Q, $161.8$~s for gated random, $146.6$~s for gated Q with SGD, $203.0$~s for cold-start Q, and $167.3$~s for direct reuse. Wall time is noisy on a shared CPU and does not strictly track update count, so updates are the more portable compute measure.

The selected states vary across seeds; no single $(H,L,D)$ dominates. This variability, together with overlapping test intervals and a 12-episode horizon that covers only a small fraction of the $18\times7$ state--action pairs, provides no convergence or local-minimum-avoidance guarantee. The defensible conclusion is consequently narrower: within this budget, Q-learning, random control, and the tested reuse policies discover architectures whose common-protocol performance is similar. A larger action space would require more search repetitions or a function approximator, not an extrapolation from the current table.

Tensor-shape agreement also remains an incomplete semantic criterion. A dropout change at $L=1$ preserves every tensor but changes no active recurrent operation, whereas a depth change invalidates the full checkpoint despite shared substructure. Partial module transfer and explicit inherited-parameter fractions are promising extensions \cite{cai2018efficient}, but they must be evaluated under the same fresh-final-training control used here.

\section{Threats to Validity and Future Work}
The revised protocol removes the most direct leakage mechanisms in the original notebook: traces are partitioned before windowing, the scaler is fitted only on training traces, search is repeated across five declared seeds, final architectures are trained under a common protocol, and the official test partition is excluded from model selection. Nevertheless, five seeds provide limited power, and overlapping confidence intervals should not be interpreted as proof of equivalence. A preregistered equivalence margin and substantially more paired repetitions would be required for such a claim.

ECG5000 also has an unusually narrow biological source. Its 5,000 beats were derived from a single severe-congestive-heart-failure record, so the official test split is unseen at the beat level but not independent at the patient level. No result in this paper establishes cross-patient, cross-device, or cross-institution generalization. The class labels are excluded from the target, and next-amplitude error is neither a diagnostic endpoint nor a clinical-benefit measure.

The one-factor-at-a-time ablations isolate the primary implementation choices more clearly than the original joint comparison, but they are not a full $2\times2\times3$ factorial experiment. The validation gate also consumes additional updates. Future work should use paired seeds, equalized total gradient budgets, additional HPO baselines such as Hyperband and BOHB, and external multi-lead cohorts. Modern forecasting architectures \cite{nie2023patchtst,wu2023timesnet,zeng2023dlinear,luo2024moderntcn} should be rerun on the identical one-step endpoint before any numerical state-of-the-art claim is made.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Ethical Scope}
The revised notebook records data URLs, split and model seeds, subset sizes, controller constants, every transition, compatibility and gate decisions, final-training histories, gradient-update counts, wall times, per-seed test metrics, and aggregate intervals. It exports \texttt{reviewer\_ablation\_episodes.csv}, \texttt{reviewer\_final\_training\_history.csv}, \texttt{reviewer\_ablation\_per\_seed.csv}, and \texttt{reviewer\_ablation\_summary.csv}. Exact replay should additionally pin Python, NumPy, scikit-learn, and PyTorch versions and record processor metadata.

The system produces neither a diagnosis nor a patient-specific recommendation. ECG5000 class labels are excluded and the target is a single standardized amplitude. Forecast error therefore has no direct interpretation as sensitivity, specificity, calibration, or safety. The method should be regarded strictly as a signal-modeling study unless evaluated against clinically defined endpoints under appropriate governance and expert review.

\section{Conclusion}
We formulated LSTM configuration as a finite Bellman search and audited checkpoint inheritance under leakage-controlled, multi-seed evaluation. After common-protocol retraining, gated Q-learning achieves test MSE $0.02355\pm0.00162$, whereas Q-learning without reuse achieves $0.02288\pm0.00092$ and uses fewer mean gradient updates. Random control, SGD-search, and direct-reuse variants occupy the same narrow performance range. The experiment therefore does not support a statistically resolved advantage for Q-learning or inheritance within the tested budget. This negative result is informative: the dramatic gain in the original notebook was not attributable to RL because optimizer, reuse, and checkpoint age changed jointly. The revised evidence supports the reproducibility of the forecasting pipeline, but broader claims require more paired seeds, budget-matched search, modern baselines on the same endpoint, and external multi-patient ECG data.
